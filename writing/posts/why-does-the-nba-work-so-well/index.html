Why does the NBA work so well?

August 2, 20265 min readThoughts

The goal of this post is first and foremost to educate, rather than convince. And the hope it makes you think.

Many people say, “Show me one example of socialism1 working.” To this, I will always use the National Basketball Association (NBA) as an example.2 Now to be clear, I am absolutely not talking about pure socialism but incorporating socialist elements.

A lot of people tend to believe that the NBA is closer to pure capitalism since the organizations are owned by billionaires, there are superstar salaries which drive the mean far higher than the median, the free market exists, etc. But the actual structure of the NBA is one of the most textbook forms of socialism that we have today.

But here’s my issue with it: the owners in the NBA who partake in this, fund politicians to tell the average working person that socialism does not work.

One of the biggest things that every NBA team participates in is revenue sharing. This means that every team will take around 50% of their revenue for that year and will throw it into a shared pot. Thus, every team that would not survive otherwise now gets to partake in that pot and get to stay afloat. If you are a big market city such as the New York Knicks or the Los Angeles Lakers, your money is directly going towards helping smaller-market teams such as the Charlotte Hornets, Utah Jazz, and Memphis Grizzlies function.

Then there is perhaps one of the most progressive taxes that you’ll ever see, which is the luxury tax — it’s a direct tax on the wealthy in the NBA. Teams that exceed the salary cap3 to pay player(s) they really want to keep must pay a tax on every dollar that is spent above that cap. The money that is collected from that tax is then distributed to the other teams. For example, in 2022, the luxury tax bill was around $481 million, of which 50% of that went directly into revenue sharing, which is certainly a significant amount of money! And as for the salary cap in general, there’s collective bargaining over the free market, so you’re not allowed to just “run free”.4

Why does socialism work for billionaire owners and not for us? They are actively collecting revenue sharing dollars while actively funding anti-labor politicians. If you think about the $481 million that was collected in luxury taxes in 2022, a lot of these owners directly benefitted from that redistribution but have the audacity to say that redistribution is un-American when they go home. The NBA only works today because the “people at the bottom” (small-market teams) are protected from being destroyed financially.

Let’s use the Los Angeles Lakers as an example; if they were to just use all the money that they have to push a team such as the Memphis Grizzlies out of the league, the problem is now that they have less teams to play. So for the Lakers, it’s beneficial for them to partake in revenue sharing to say that they deem smaller market teams such as the Memphis Grizzlies, or the Charlotte Hornets, etc. valuable enough to share the wealth with.

So my question then is, why doesn’t America view their poor as valuable enough to protect?

Others will use state socialism as a definition, which is how Mao’s China and the USSR operated, which is just the government owning major industry and centrally planning the economy. You may hear democratic socialism, which has the same goal as classical socialism, where there is social or worker ownership, but it is achieved through the means of elections, rather than outright revolution. You may also hear the Nordic definition, which is a social democracy where capitalism remains the foundation but is paired with high taxes, as well as strong public services and safety nets. You may hear market socialism, where workers still own the businesses but they’re competing within the market. You may also hear libertarian socialism, which heavily emphasizes mutual aid; it features collective ownership, but with little to no state regulation. ↩
