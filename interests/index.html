  • Games
    • Set
      • A card game played with a special deck of 34 = 81 cards where each card has four features that distinguish it from the others: color, shape, shading, and number. For each feature, there are three variants. From the 12 (or occasionally more) cards that are dealt, your aim is to select a set which is a combination of three cards such that for each of the four features, the variants of that feature expressed by the three cards are either all the same or all different. This repeats until the deck is depleted.
      • This game is a great way to visualize the finite field F43.
      • I hold the world record in 7 of the 16 game modes, including the base variant where I have completed a 24-set game in 23.25 seconds and Set-Chain, where I have completed a 34-set game in 27.85 seconds.
    • ProSet
      • Another derived SET variant with a deck of 26 − 1 = 63 cards. Each card has 6 bits which are represented by the existence of a colored dot or a lack thereof. These cards may have any combination of the dots except for the fully blank card, i.e. none of the dots show. From the 7 cards that are dealt, your goal is to pick a set of cards (at least 3 cards, why?) such that the total number of dots of each color in the selected set is even (possibly 0) in the set. This repeats until the deck is depleted.
      • Bonus question: what finite vector space does ProSet represent?
    • Hanabi
      • A cooperative card game where you see everyone’s cards except their own. You give clues by color or rank to your teammates so they learn the identity of their cards, and they give you clues so you learn your cards. The goal is to play cards in an ordered stack like in solitaire — the default is 5 stacks of 5 different colors of cards ranked 1 through 5, but this can change in other variants. The user SpinTop and I achieved a 31-second two-player five-suit speedrun 4 years ago, which was 3rd fastest at the time and is still in the top 10.

Big Questions

Here are a few topics I enjoy thinking about:

  • Can we establish a framework behind creative endeavors such as ideation and collaboration? Why do contemporary AI systems feel like such a letdown in these processes? Can their deficiencies be resolved through improved Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) paradigms or do they represent an inherent limitation of cognitive offloading?
  • What environments empower human potential to fully blossom? How can creative expression and digital tools serve this growth, and could private, tailored AI systems and participatory storytelling act as catalysts?
  • Beyond vague clichés such as UBI and post-scarcity, what does a positive technological future look like? How is power distributed? How do citizens form community and human connection? How does social order remain stable and what prevents the system from collapsing into dystopia? What steps bridge today to that reality?
  • How can we make mathematics more equitable? Who tells the story of math? Who gets invited in? As for those who received access to induce change, what role should math play in society?
