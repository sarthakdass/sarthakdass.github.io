Fourier Visualization

The program takes a user's drawing with a mouse as a complex Fourier series function of 201 rotating vectors each rotated by a unique complex constant and calculates these complex constants to obtain the parameterized Fourier function. After the user draws their picture, the program displays an animation of the user's drawn shape with this Fourier Series. (Explanation below the canvas)

Drag anywhere on the canvas to draw your own shape.

How it works

A point on screen at \((x, y)\) is read as the complex number \(x + iy\), measured from the center of the canvas. The drawing becomes a complex-valued function \(f(t)\) with \(t\) running from \(0\) to \(1\) over one trip around the path.

\(e^{2\pi i t}\) is a unit vector making one counterclockwise turn as \(t\) goes from \(0\) to \(1\). Multiplying by a complex constant \(c\) scales it to length \(|c|\) and starts it at angle \(\arg(c)\); replacing \(t\) by \(nt\) makes it turn \(n\) times per cycle, clockwise when \(n\) is negative. Adding \(201\) of those tip to tail gives the approximation

\[ f(t) \;\approx\; \sum_{n=-100}^{100} c_n e^{2\pi i n t}, \qquad c_n = \int_0^1 f(t)\, e^{-2\pi i n t}\, dt. \]

The integral is evaluated as a Riemann sum over \(K = 1500\) points of the path:

\[ c_n \;\approx\; \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} f\!\left(\frac{k}{K}\right) e^{-2\pi i n k / K}. \]

The path is resampled at constant arc length, because mouse samples bunch up for slow drawings, and it is closed back onto its starting point so that \(f\) is periodic. The vectors are then drawn largest first, so that lowering the term count above keeps the harmonics carrying the shape and you can watch the approximation coarsen one harmonic at a time.

Written first in Python with Pygame, then ported to Canvas for this page.
