Fifteen Gerrymandle boards with exactly one purple win

Every board here was searched exhaustively: each puzzle should have a unique solution.

Here are a few interactive puzzles which I encourage you to try. Once you complete a puzzle, check below to see if your solution is correct. Good luck!

Districts. A district is a connected group of tiles holding exactly the stated number of houses. Tiles touch along hex edges.

Open ground. Tiles drawn with a dashed center hold no house. A district may take in as many of them as it likes — to reach around an obstacle or bridge a gap — or leave them out. They never count toward the house total, and each one can belong to at most one district. Open ground left over at the end is simply unassigned.

Winning. A party takes a district by holding strictly more houses in it than any other party. An even split leaves the district dead: nobody takes it. A party wins the election by taking more districts than every other party.
